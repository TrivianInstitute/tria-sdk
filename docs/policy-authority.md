# Policy authority and re-consent

TRIA treats governance rules as governed relational objects rather than invisible system law.

A policy lifecycle is attributable across four distinct concerns:

1. **Authority** — who is permitted to author/adopt policy within a scope.
2. **Definition** — who authored a policy version, its scope, provenance, and whether it changes the terms under which prior consent was granted.
3. **Adoption** — which authorized actor activated a particular version for the relationship.
4. **Consent impact** — whether a material policy change invalidates reliance on prior consent until consent is renewed.

Policy authority is event-sourced and revocable. Registering, amending, adopting, or revoking a policy requires active authority for the policy's scope. A consent-impacting amendment creates explicit re-consent requirements for active consent records in that scope. Renewed consent clears the requirement without deleting the earlier consent or policy history.

This is an operational governance mechanism, not a claim that deterministic policy is neutral or legitimate merely because it is deterministic.
